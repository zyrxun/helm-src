# Landing-Page Feedback Requests — Honest Pre-Launch Outreach

> Status: **DRAFT — founder review required before any post.**
> Voice + pricing rules per `public/brand/brand.md`. Founder posts from a
> personal account and replies to every comment.
>
> **The one rule that makes this work: always own the page.** Every post
> discloses "I built this." This is the line between legitimate feedback and
> astroturfing — posing as a neutral third party ("which of these two sites is
> better?" while hiding that it's yours) gets detected, gets you banned, and
> torches the get-helm.app brand right before launch. Disclosed = welcomed;
> disguised = off-brand and a reputational landmine. We don't do the disguised
> version.

## What this channel is (and isn't)

It's an honest request for design/conversion feedback on the real pre-launch
page — which doubles as soft exposure. It is **not** an acquisition channel:
the people who critique landing pages are designers, devs, and founders, not
Mac power users. So the **primary yield is page improvements; signups are a
side effect.** Budget the effort accordingly.

Good fit for the founder specifically: this is a conversation about the *page*
(hero, CTA, clarity), not the code — zero deep-technical Q&A, which sidesteps
the no-live-technical-formats constraint (`marketing/founder-technical-faq.md`).

## Timing & dependencies

- **Posting reveals the product publicly → wait until after the Jun 18 reveal.**
- **Get feedback on the FINAL page → after the site rebuild.** Critiquing a page
  you're about to replace wastes the one post you get per community.
- **Window: ~Jun 19–25**, so changes can actually ship before the Jul 1 launch.
- Pre-write the posts + pick venues now; post once reveal + rebuild are done.

## Venues — subreddits (researched Jun 14) + non-Reddit

**Read each sub's current rules first.** Two things get posts removed/banned:
- Many require a **designated thread** (Showoff Saturday, Share Your Startup, a
  feedback megathread) — a standalone "review my site" outside those is removed.
- The **9:1 rule** (≈9 genuine non-promo interactions per self-post) applies on
  r/webdev and broadly, so you need some prior participation/karma.
- **Reciprocate** — critique others' pages too. It's the etiquette on the design
  subs and the reputational safeguard.

**Tier 1 — critique-focused (best for fixing the page):**
- **r/design_critiques** — purpose-built "post your design for critique." Best for hero/CTA/visual clarity.
- **r/RoastMyStartup** — blunt landing-page + concept roasts; founder audience.
- **r/web_design** — design critique welcomed; reciprocity expected.
- **r/UXDesign** — UX/conversion clarity ("does the hero say what it does in 5s?").

**Tier 2 — maker/founder feedback + incidental exposure (the "both" goal):**
- **r/SideProject** — "I built this, feedback?"; some Mac users. (Also in SCHEDULE.)
- **r/SaaS** — landing-page feedback common; founder audience.
- **r/EntrepreneurRideAlong** — feedback-friendly, less strict than r/Entrepreneur.
- **r/alphaandbetausers** — early adopters/testers; usability feedback. (Also in SCHEDULE.)
- **r/growmybusiness** — sharing + feedback allowed.

**Tier 3 — dev/founder, designated threads only (strict self-promo):**
- **r/webdev** — 9:1 rule; share in "Showoff Saturday" only.
- **r/Frontend** — smaller front-end community.
- **r/startups** — "Share Your Startup" thread only.
- **r/Entrepreneur** — feedback in designated threads only.

**Non-Reddit:** **Indie Hackers** ("I built this" / feedback), **Peerlist**
project feedback, designer/maker **Discords/Slacks** you're already in.

Three things I'd genuinely like eyes on:

## Rules

- **Own the page in every post.** Never pose as a neutral party. Non-negotiable.
- One post per community; reply to every comment; never ask for upvotes.
- Follow each venue's feedback-thread / self-promo rules (designated threads,
  9:1, karma gates) — verify them the day you post.
- Reciprocate on the design subs.
- Keep voice per brand.md: no exclamation points, present tense, no filler.

## Watched number

**Concrete page changes shipped from the feedback** (primary). Referral signups
from these venues (secondary). Five usable critiques that change the page beat
fifty "looks great" comments.

## Note — "which format is better" with rigor

These posts give you fast *qualitative* direction. If you want a data-backed
answer to which format converts, that's an **A/B test with analytics** — a
post-launch option, since the site currently has **no analytics** (it was in the
new-site spec, not yet added). Don't block the feedback posts on it.
